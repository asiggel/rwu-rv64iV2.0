Variant~A uses X-FAB \textbf{XH018} 180\,nm (0.18\,\textmu m) technology with a
4-metal stack. It is the variant prepared for the first tapeout.

XH018 is X-FAB's 0.18\,\textmu m modular mixed-signal CMOS technology.
The digital core runs at the nominal 1.8\,V core supply; the I/O ring uses
3.3\,V I/O cells.
The standard-cell library is \texttt{D\_CELLS\_HD} (1.8\,V, high density),
the I/O library \texttt{IO\_CELLS\_3V}. Both are used in the LPMOS variant
on the 4-metal stack, which is the variant the SRAM macros are generated for.

On-chip SRAM (I-Mem and D-Mem) is generated with the XH018 SPRAM compilers
from the X-FAB design-kit portal (Section~\ref{sec:archtech}).
The compiler deliverables are covered by an NDA; this specification therefore
gives only rounded total areas.

\paragraph{Reason for XH018.}
The selection is driven by cost. For a multi-project-wafer (MPW) run the price
scales with the die area, and in Variant~A the die area is dominated by the
40\,KiB of on-chip SRAM:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The per-mm\textsuperscript{2} MPW price of XH018 (4-metal) is about
    1.5$\times$ that of XH035 (Europractice price list 2026).
  \item The area of standard cells and SRAM is about 3--4$\times$ smaller in XH018.
  \item As a result, the same design costs roughly half as much in XH018.
\end{itemize}

All SRAM macros use only the lower three metal layers plus the top metal, so
the cheapest 4-metal stack is sufficient.
